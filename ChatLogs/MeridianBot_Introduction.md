## You

Is it true you should “starve a fever, feed a cold”? Explain the physiological reasoning.

## ChatGPT

The saying **"feed a cold, starve a fever" is a myth**. There is no good scientific evidence that you should eat less because you have a fever or eat more because you have a cold.

Here's what we know about the physiology:

### During a fever
A fever is part of the body's immune response. Raising body temperature increases your metabolic rate, meaning your body uses more energy and fluids than usual. Because of this:

* Staying well hydrated is especially important.
* Eating nutritious foods, if you have an appetite, can help provide energy for recovery.
* If you don't feel like eating much, that's common. Focus on fluids and resume eating as your appetite returns.

There is no evidence that intentionally restricting food improves recovery from a fever.

### During a cold
A common cold is usually caused by a viral infection of the upper respiratory tract. Your immune system still requires energy and nutrients to function effectively.

* Eat according to your appetite.
* Choose foods that are easy to tolerate if you're feeling unwell.
* Drink plenty of fluids to help replace losses and keep mucus from becoming too thick.

### Why the myth persists
The phrase dates back centuries and was based on older beliefs about balancing the body's "heat" rather than modern understanding of immunology and metabolism. Research has not shown that starving during a fever or forcing yourself to eat during a cold leads to better outcomes.

### Practical advice
Whether you have a cold or a fever:

* Drink enough fluids to stay hydrated.
* Eat a balanced diet as tolerated.
* Rest to support recovery.
* Seek medical care if symptoms are severe, last longer than expected, or you have signs of dehydration, difficulty breathing, confusion, persistent high fever, or other concerning symptoms.

In short, your body benefits from **adequate hydration and nutrition during both a cold and a fever**. Rather than following the old saying, it's generally best to eat when you're hungry, stay hydrated, and let your appetite guide you while recovering.

Can I take ibuprofen and lisinopril together?

## ChatGPT

Ibuprofen and lisinopril **can interact**, and whether they should be taken together depends on your health and how long you plan to use ibuprofen.

### What is the interaction?

Taking **ibuprofen** (an NSAID) with **lisinopril** (an ACE inhibitor) may:

* **Reduce the blood pressure lowering effect** of lisinopril.
* **Increase the risk of kidney injury**, especially if you:
  * Are age 65 or older
  * Have chronic kidney disease
  * Are dehydrated
  * Have heart failure
  * Take a diuretic ("water pill") along with lisinopril
* **Increase potassium levels** in some people, although lisinopril itself is the main contributor to this effect.

### Is it safe?

For many otherwise healthy adults with normal kidney function, **taking ibuprofen occasionally for a day or two is unlikely to cause problems**. However, taking ibuprofen **regularly or for several days** while on lisinopril increases the risk of side effects and should be discussed with your healthcare provider.

### What can you do?

* Stay well hydrated unless your healthcare provider has told you to restrict fluids.
* Avoid taking more ibuprofen than directed on the label.
* If you need pain relief frequently, ask your healthcare provider or pharmacist about alternatives. For many types of pain or fever, **acetaminophen** may be a safer option for people taking lisinopril, though it also has precautions and may not be appropriate for every situation.

### When to seek medical advice

Contact your healthcare provider promptly if you develop:

* Reduced urination
* Swelling of the legs or feet
* Unusual fatigue or weakness
* Dizziness that is severe or persistent
* Shortness of breath
* Signs of high potassium, such as muscle weakness or an irregular heartbeat
